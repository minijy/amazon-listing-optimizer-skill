# Compliance and claims guardrails

Read this reference for regulated products, certifications, safety claims, environmental claims, or any content with legal or account-suspension risk.

## Evidence hierarchy

From strongest to weakest:

1. Applicable law, regulator authorization, and current Amazon marketplace/category policy.
2. Product label, certification, accredited test report, technical specification, and signed manufacturer documentation.
3. Seller-owned first-party performance data with a documented method.
4. Customer research used for language and concerns, not as proof of product performance.
5. Competitor pages and generic web content, which are discovery inputs only.

Do not draft a factual claim from levels 4–5. Store the evidence source and scope for each high-risk claim.

## Never invent or silently strengthen

- Dimensions, materials, capacity, power, performance duration, compatibility, package quantity, origin, warranty, certifications, test results, or safety ratings.
- “Clinically proven,” “FDA approved,” “non-toxic,” “hypoallergenic,” “organic,” “biodegradable,” “carbon neutral,” “BPA-free,” or similar regulated/technical language.
- Disease diagnosis, cure, mitigation, treatment, or prevention claims.
- Absolute or unbounded claims such as “100% safe,” “works for everyone,” “never,” “permanent,” or “guaranteed.”
- “Best,” “#1,” “top rated,” endorsements, awards, sales rank, or comparative superiority without acceptable current substantiation.

If evidence is incomplete, replace the claim with a neutral verified fact or mark `[NEEDS EVIDENCE]`. A disclaimer does not repair a prohibited or misleading claim.

## Listing-specific policy risks

- Do not include prices, discounts, coupons, urgency, shipping promises, refund guarantees, contact details, URLs, QR codes, or calls to transact elsewhere in catalog content.
- Do not quote or summarize customer reviews as seller claims unless the marketplace explicitly permits the use and the claim remains substantiated.
- Do not include competitor trademarks, ASINs, copyrighted text, or copied image concepts as keyword bait.
- Do not add and remove prohibited claims to evade enforcement.
- Keep parent and child variation content accurate; do not make one child inherit facts that only apply to another.
- Do not describe accessories, props, or package contents that are not included.

## High-risk category routing

For supplements, food, cosmetics, medical devices, pesticides, children’s products, batteries, chemicals, PPE, automotive safety products, and products making environmental claims:

1. Ask for the target marketplace and exact category.
2. Request the current category policy or verify it from Amazon before final copy.
3. Request labels, certifications, test reports, and claim substantiation.
4. Separate permitted structure/function or descriptive language from disease or guaranteed-outcome claims.
5. Return a compliance review queue; do not represent the result as legal approval.

## Output requirement

For each risky statement, return:

| Proposed statement | Risk type | Evidence required | Status | Safer fallback |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Use `approved`, `needs evidence`, or `remove`; never use “probably compliant.”
