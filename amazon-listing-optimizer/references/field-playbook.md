# Field playbook

Use this reference when creating or rewriting listing fields. Verify the marketplace and category template before treating a general limit as binding.

## Title

Goal: identify the exact offer in a scan and earn a relevant click.

Recommended information order, adjusted for category conventions:

`Brand + product type + model/line + decisive attribute + size/color/quantity + compatibility or use qualifier`

- Front-load identity and the strongest truthful differentiator.
- Use natural syntax; a long title is not a keyword container.
- Amazon’s January 2025 general policy says most categories cap titles at 200 characters, prohibit `! $ ? _ { } ^ ¬ ¦` unless part of a brand name, and prohibit most words appearing more than twice.
- Amazon advertising guidance often recommends approximately 60 characters to reduce truncation; newer seller guidance recommends 80 or fewer as a practical target. These are recommendations, not universal category limits.
- Avoid subjective claims, promotional phrases, seller information, decorative punctuation, and variation details that do not apply to every child represented.

## Bullet points

Goal: answer the five questions most likely to determine purchase or return.

Default sequence:

1. Primary outcome and mechanism.
2. Decisive specification or performance boundary.
3. Fit, compatibility, dimensions, or package contents.
4. Operation, care, installation, or use scenario.
5. Expectation-setting, material, safety, or relevant limitation.

Use a short benefit-oriented lead-in only when it improves scanning. Keep one decision theme per bullet. Do not force five bullets when the category exposes fewer fields.

Amazon’s August 2024 update restricts special characters, emojis, refund-related guarantees, and other non-compliant phrases. Amazon Ads recommends at least three clear bullets and avoiding pricing or promotional information.

## Description

Goal: act as a concise buying guide rather than repeat the bullets.

- State who it is for, the problem it addresses, how it works, and what is included.
- Explain setup, care, compatibility, boundaries, and use cases that need full sentences.
- Use original copy. Do not copy a manufacturer or competitor description.
- Follow category-specific character and HTML rules; they vary.
- When A+ Content replaces or suppresses the standard description in a storefront, still maintain accurate catalog content.

## Backend search terms

Goal: cover relevant language omitted from visible fields.

- Use single spaces rather than commas or semicolons.
- Prefer synonyms, alternate names, abbreviations, regional terms, and supported use cases.
- Omit duplicates already present in title, bullets, or description when possible.
- Omit stop words when space is constrained; Amazon accounts for capitalization, common misspellings, and pluralization.
- Never include competitor brands, ASINs, subjective claims such as “best,” irrelevant traffic terms, or prohibited claims.
- Do not assume a universal byte limit; inspect the current marketplace/category field.

## Images

Treat the main image and secondary images differently.

### Main image

- Show only the product and included components that are accurate for the offer.
- Use a clean white background when required by the category.
- Make the product easy to recognize at thumbnail size.
- Do not add badges, text overlays, borders, watermarks, props that are not included, or misleading scale.

Amazon Ads recommends at least 1,000 px on a side for zoom and says the product should fill at least 80% of the frame. Confirm the current category image rules before upload.

### Secondary image sequence

1. Hero use case and primary outcome.
2. Mechanism or differentiator with evidence.
3. Dimensions, scale, fit, or compatibility.
4. Package contents or installation steps.
5. Secondary use case or audience.
6. Objection, limitation, care, or safety clarification.
7. Brand story or comparison within the seller’s own product family.

Each image should answer one purchase question. Do not make visual claims that the text could not lawfully make.

## A+ Content

- Use modules to educate and prove, not to repeat title and bullets.
- Build a narrative: problem/context → mechanism → proof → use cases → comparison → expectation setting.
- Compare only products the seller has the right to reference; avoid competitor disparagement.
- Provide meaningful image alt text where supported.
- Treat A+ availability and module rules as account/marketplace dependent.

## Localization

- Re-research local search language; do not translate a US keyword list literally.
- Convert units and decimal conventions while retaining technically accurate source values.
- Adapt regulated claims, plugs/voltage, sizing standards, compatibility, package language, and cultural use cases.
- Preserve brand/model names and legally required wording.
- Produce a marketplace-specific keyword map and flag facts needing local compliance review.
